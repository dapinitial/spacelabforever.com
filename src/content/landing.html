The moat · measurement-assisted sync

Everyone else's room tuning is locked to their ecosystem. Ours is locked to your couch.

Sonos Trueplay does this for Sonos only. Dirac does it for $499 and a pro AVR. We do it across protocols, from the Mac you already own.

Your Mac's mic is the reference.

Press Play All. SuperAudio plays a brief chirp from each speaker, measures how long the sound takes to reach where you sit, and pulls AirPlay close to Sonos automatically. Then you set a static offset and nudge it by ear — usually a one-time tweak, because your ears at the couch are the final judge, not the mic's spot.

Signal check · FAQ

The questions everyone asks.

Q1 How is this different from Airfoil? ›

Airfoil ($35) is a solid 20-year-old Mac app. SuperAudio is roughly half the price, adds measurement-assisted cross-protocol room tuning no consumer product offers, and is built specifically for the stranded old hardware — gen-1 Sonos, B&W A5/A7, AirPort Express.

Q2 Does the music actually stay in sync between rooms? ›

Yes, with a one-time tune. AirPlay 1 syncs sample-accurately on a shared clock; Sonos has a hidden buffer we measure with your Mac's mic. You set a static offset and nudge by ear to lock it at your seat. Fully hands-free continuous correction is in research.

Q3 What about AirPlay 2 and my HomePods? ›

We built a full AirPlay 2 sender and verified it against real Apple hardware — but Apple's FairPlay DRM gates the audio on modern receivers, so we've parked it rather than ship something that doesn't play. Modern Sonos is unaffected (it uses Sonos' own protocol). HomePods are the one speaker this currently keeps out.

Q4 My old Sonos keeps getting demoted by Sonos. Still works? ›

SuperAudio drives any Sonos that still speaks UPnP — which is every model ever shipped, including the ones Sonos pushed off their main app. We don't depend on Sonos' cloud or app.

Q5 Privacy? ›

Everything is on your LAN. No cloud, no account, no telemetry, no analytics. Discovery is local-only. The app never phones home.
